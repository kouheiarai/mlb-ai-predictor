## The beat

The spine of this newsletter is **where Japanese money is moving** — the roughly two-quadrillion-yen
pool of household financial assets, and what is happening to it. Japanese households have held an
unusually large share of their wealth in cash and deposits for decades. That is changing. The change
is one of the largest capital reallocations in the world, and almost nobody outside Japan is
tracking it week to week.

Concretely, the recurring subject matter is:

- Household financial assets: the Bank of Japan's quarterly flow of funds accounts, the cash-versus-
  securities split, and how it is shifting.
- NISA (Japan's tax-advantaged retail investment account, expanded in 2024): account openings,
  inflows, what people are actually buying, and where those flows land — domestic equities, US index
  funds, or elsewhere.
- Retail investor behaviour: brokerage account data, fund flows, the mix of domestic versus foreign
  allocation, generational differences.
- Institutional money that shapes the same picture: the Government Pension Investment Fund, life
  insurers, corporate cash and buybacks, and cross-border portfolio flows.
- Policy that moves any of the above: FSA rules, tax treatment, corporate governance reform,
  pension design.
- Inflation and wages, but only through the lens of household balance sheets — what real returns on
  cash actually look like now.
