# Special edition — Bank of Japan policy meeting

Today is {{TODAY}}. The Bank of Japan's Monetary Policy Meeting concluded on {{BOJ_MEETING_DATE}}.
Write the special edition covering it.

## What makes this edition different

Every outlet reports the rate decision within minutes. Nobody outside Japan reads the statement
carefully, and almost nobody compares it to the previous one. **That comparison is the product.**

The BOJ signals its intentions by changing individual words and phrases in an otherwise near-identical
statement. A single adjective moving from "gradually" to "steadily", or a clause on inflation
expectations being added or dropped, is how the policy shift is actually communicated. Traders who
can read Japanese know this. Your readers cannot read Japanese. That asymmetry is your entire edge.

## What to do

1. **Retrieve the current statement** from the Bank of Japan's own site (boj.or.jp). Get the Japanese
   original, not only the English translation — the English version is an official translation but
   the nuance and the timing both live in the Japanese.
2. **Retrieve the previous meeting's statement** from the same source.
3. **Diff them.** Identify every substantive change in wording. Ignore pure formatting and date
   changes.
4. **For each meaningful change, explain what it signals.** What was the old formulation, what is the
   new one, and what does the shift imply about the Bank's reaction function?
5. **Report the vote.** The split matters — who dissented and in which direction is often more
   informative than the headline decision. Name the dissenters and what they proposed.
6. **Cover the Outlook Report** if one was released at this meeting (they come quarterly), including
   the revisions to the growth and inflation projections against the previous round.
7. **Report the market reaction** — yen, JGB yields, the Nikkei — and, separately, what analysts
   attributed it to. Keep those two things clearly distinguished.
8. **Connect it back to households**, which is this newsletter's spine: what does this decision mean
   for the real return on the enormous pool of cash Japanese households hold?

## Structure for this edition

Use the standard structure from your instructions, with one addition: include a section titled
**"What changed in the statement"** containing a simple two-column HTML table — previous wording on
the left, new wording on the right — followed by a short paragraph on each change that matters.
Quote the English official translation in the table, and note where the Japanese original carries a
nuance the translation loses.

## Accuracy

Quote wording exactly as published. Do not paraphrase a phrase and present it as a quote. If you
cannot retrieve the previous statement, say so plainly and write the edition without the diff table
rather than reconstructing it from memory — a reconstructed diff is worse than no diff.
