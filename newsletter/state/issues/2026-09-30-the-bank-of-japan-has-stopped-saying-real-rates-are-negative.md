# The Bank of Japan has stopped saying real rates are negative

*It raised to 1.25% on a 7-2 vote, and the newest member dissented at her first meeting. The wording changed more than the rate did.*

- 配信日: 2026-09-30
- 号種: boj

---

## レビュー用サマリー（日本語・配信されません）

**この号の主張**

9月18日、日銀は政策金利を1.0％から1.25％に引き上げた。**しかしこの号の主題は0.25ポイントではなく、声明文の文言変更である。** 6月の「実質金利は……マイナスとなっている」が、9月には「なお低い水準となっている」に変わった。日銀が自ら「実質金利はマイナス」と言わなくなったということで、緩和的である根拠の書き方が変わっている。もう一つ、物価上振れリスクの原因が「原油価格上昇を起点とした価格転嫁」から「企業の賃金・価格設定行動の積極化」に付け替えられた。輸入インフレから国内発のインフレへ、という説明の転換。加えて票決が7対1から7対2に割れ、**新任の佐藤綾野委員が初回の会合で反対している。**

**確信度が低い箇所**

- **市場の反応だけが二次情報。** 円が158円台、日経平均が約500円高という数字は日経・ピクテ等の報道による。日銀や取引所の一次データでは確認していない。本文でも報道に帰属させている。
- 「タカ派色が乏しく円安で反応した」という解釈は市場参加者の見方であり、本稿の主張ではない。本文で「アナリストがそう説明している」と明記した。
- 中川順子委員が6月の出席者にいて9月にいない点は、両声明の出席欄から確認できる事実だが、**退任の経緯・時期は確認していない。** 本文では佐藤委員が新任である点だけを書き、中川委員には触れていない。
- 9月会合では展望レポートは公表されていない（7月分を参照している）。本文でもそう書いた。
- 文言比較は公式英訳どうしで行った。日本語原文でも同じ変化（「マイナス」→「なお低い水準」）を確認済み。

**コンプライアンス自己点検**

- **金利の先行き予想を自分ではしていない。** 「引き続き政策金利を引き上げ……」は日銀自身の文言として引用しており、筆者の予測としては書いていない。次の利上げ時期には触れていない。
- 個別銘柄・商品の推奨なし。預金・住宅ローンは過去号の数字を再掲して影響を説明しているだけで、「固定にすべき」等は書いていない。
- 反対した2委員の主張は、声明の（注）の記述に沿って要約した。各委員の人物評はしていない。
- 為替・株の見通しなし。当日の値動きと、それに対する市場参加者の説明を、帰属付きで書いただけ。

<h3>The deleted word</h3>

<p>Start with the first row, because it is the one that will not be reported anywhere else.</p>

<p>For years the Bank's argument for why policy remained accommodative even as the nominal rate rose was that the <em>real</em> rate &mdash; nominal minus expected inflation &mdash; was still below zero. Negative real rates mean money is effectively free. It was the load-bearing sentence in the case for going slowly.</p>

<p>In September that sentence is gone. Real rates are now described as "at low levels". The Japanese original makes the same move: <em>&#12510;&#12452;&#12490;&#12473;</em> becomes <em>&#12394;&#12362;&#20302;&#12356;&#27700;&#28310;</em>.</p>

<p>Low is not negative. The Bank has not announced that policy has stopped being accommodative &mdash; it says in the next breath that accommodative conditions will be maintained. But it has quietly retired the strongest version of that claim, and it did so in the same statement in which it raised rates.</p>

<h3>The cause of inflation was reassigned</h3>

<p>In June the risk of overshooting 2% was blamed on oil: crude prices up, costs passing through business to business, possibly reaching consumers. That is imported inflation, and it is the kind that fades.</p>

<p>In September the same risk is attributed to "firms' behavior shifting more toward raising wages and prices." That is domestic, and it is the kind that does not fade, because it is a habit rather than a shock.</p>

<p>A central bank that thinks inflation is oil can wait. A central bank that thinks inflation is how companies now behave has less reason to.</p>

<h2>The vote split wider</h2>

<p>June was 7&ndash;1. September was <strong>7&ndash;2</strong>.</p>

<p>Asada Toichiro dissented both times, but for different stated reasons. In June it was the Middle East: downside risks to production and employment outweighed upside risks to prices. In September he argued instead that CPI excluding fresh food is running below 2% and "it could not necessarily be said that the economic situation was strong."</p>

<p>The second dissent is the one to notice. <strong>Sato Ayano voted against at her first meeting on the board</strong>, on the grounds that economic and price developments had not accelerated enough to justify moving now.</p>

<p>One more detail from the attendance list, which nobody reads: Governor Ueda was <em>absent</em> from the June meeting, which Deputy Governor Himino chaired. He was back in the chair in September.</p>

<h2>The market read it as dovish</h2>

<p>Here is the part that looks wrong until you see why.</p>

<p>The Bank raised rates, and the yen <em>fell</em> &mdash; to the 158 level against the dollar on the evening of the 18th, <a href="https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUB1809Y0Y6A910C2000000/">according to Nikkei</a>. The Nikkei 225 rose several hundred points.</p>

<p>Analysts quoted in the Japanese coverage attribute this to two things: the hike itself was fully priced in, and the two dissenting votes read as a signal that the next one is not close. On that reading, a rate rise with a widening split inside the board is less hawkish than a smaller move agreed unanimously.</p>

<p>I would add that the wording changes point the other way, and the market does not appear to have priced them at all.</p>

<h2>What it means for a household</h2>

<p>Three things follow, and this newsletter has the numbers for all of them.</p>

<p><strong>Deposits.</strong> The megabank ordinary account went to 0.40% in August, set when the policy rate was 1.0%. At 1.25% there is room for another move. Whether the banks hand it over at the same fraction is the open question &mdash; they passed through well under half last time.</p>

<p><strong>Retail government bonds.</strong> The rate on the retail JGB is a formula off the base rate, so it rises mechanically. The variable ten-year still pays two-thirds of it.</p>

<p><strong>Mortgages.</strong> Three-quarters of Japan's recent borrowers are on variable rates. Most of them will notice nothing: the five-year rule freezes the monthly payment regardless. The second rate rise of 2026 will show up in their principal balance, not their bank statement, and not for years.</p>

<p>The Bank says it will "continue to raise the policy interest rate." Two of its nine members now disagree about the pace. Both of those facts are in the same document, and the household is on the receiving end of whichever one wins.</p>
