# The Bank of Japan says it has hit its target. It did not raise rates.

*It held at 1.25% on an 8-1 vote, the lone dissenter wanted 1.5%, and the pace of its equity selling more than doubled.*

- 配信日: 2026-11-04
- 号種: boj

---

## レビュー用サマリー（日本語・配信されません）

**この号の主張**

10月30日、日銀は政策金利を1.25％に据え置いた（賛成8・反対1）。**しかし同時に出た展望レポートで、基調的な物価上昇率の表現が「2％に近づいている」から「2％の目標とおおむね整合的な水準にある」に変わった。事実上の目標達成宣言。** それでいて金利は動かさず、ETFの売却ペースを年3,300億円→8,600億円（簿価、時価で約2兆4,600億円）に引き上げた。票決も反転していて、9月に利上げへ反対した浅田・佐藤の両委員が今回は多数派に回り、唯一の反対は**1.5％への利上げを提案した高田委員**。さらに、展望レポートは「中長期の予想物価上昇率は2％程度」としているが、**9号目で扱った日銀自身の生活意識アンケートでは、家計の5年後予想は平均10.1％・中央値5.0％**。同じ銀行が17日違いで出した2つの数字が噛み合っていない。

**確信度が低い箇所**

- **「事実上の目標達成宣言」は本稿の解釈。** 日銀は「達成した」とは書いておらず、「おおむね整合的な水準にある」と書いている。ただし高田委員の反対理由が「基調物価が『物価安定の目標』に達した中」と明記しているので、政策委員自身がそう読んでいることは示せる。本文でもその順序で書いた。
- 9月の反対2名（浅田・佐藤）が今回多数派に回った点は、両会合の（注）の賛否名簿から確認した事実。**ただし両委員が考えを変えたのか、議案の中身（利上げか据え置きか）が変わったから賛否が変わっただけなのかは、資料からは分からない。** 本文でも「議案が変わった」という可能性を併記している。
- 時価換算の約2兆4,600億円は日銀の脚注の数字で、本年1〜9月の実績比率に基づく試算。今後の株価次第で変わる。
- 消費税減税は**国会で審議中**で成立していない。日銀も見通しに織り込んでいない。本文でも「審議中」と明記した。
- 展望レポートの数値はすべて政策委員見通しの**中央値**。レンジは別にある。本文では中央値であることを書いた。
- 「予想物価上昇率2％程度」と家計調査の10.1％の並置について。日銀が参照する予想物価上昇率は市場・企業・家計など複数の指標の総体であり、脚注4も「various indicators…many of these indicators are at around 2 percent」としている。**家計調査だけを見ていないのは当然で、日銀が矛盾しているという書き方はしていない。** 「どの指標を見ているかで答えが変わる」という書き方にした。

**コンプライアンス自己点検**

- 金利の先行き予想なし。高田委員の提案は議事上の事実として書き、筆者の見通しとしては書いていない。
- 株価の予想なし。ETF売却の規模は事実として書き、市場への影響は「日銀自身が市場全体の売買代金比で説明している」という形にとどめた。**「株を売るべき／買うべき」に類する示唆はない。**
- 個別銘柄・商品の推奨なし。
- 消費税減税の是非について評価していない。見通しに織り込まれていないという事実のみ。
- 日銀批判になっていないか確認した。指標の選び方の話であって、誤りの指摘ではない。

## 本文（この HTML を beehiiv に貼る）

```html
<p>The Bank of Japan met on 29 and 30 October and left the policy rate at 1.25%. On the face of it, nothing happened.</p>

<p>Three things happened.</p>

<h2>What changed in the language</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/outlook/gor2610a.pdf">October Outlook Report</a> carries the Bank's assessment of where inflation actually is. Set it against the September statement this newsletter went through five weeks ago.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Underlying inflation, September</strong> &mdash; "underlying CPI inflation has been <strong>approaching</strong> 2 percent"</li>
  <li><strong>Underlying inflation, October</strong> &mdash; "underlying CPI inflation is at a level that is <strong>generally consistent with</strong> the price stability target of 2 percent"</li>
  <li><strong>The supporting footnote, new in October</strong> &mdash; across indicators for core CPI, inflation expectations and trend inflation, "many of these indicators are at around 2 percent"</li>
</ul>

<p>Approaching became arrived. After roughly three decades of trying, the Bank is saying the 2% target is met.</p>

<p>And then it did not raise rates.</p>

<h2>The dissent changed sides</h2>

<p>September's vote was 7&ndash;2, both dissenters arguing the Bank should <em>not</em> have raised. <a href="https://www.boj.or.jp/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k261030a.pdf">October's was 8&ndash;1</a>, and the one holdout wanted the opposite.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Asada and Sato</strong> &mdash; dissented in September against raising &middot; voted with the majority in October</li>
  <li><strong>Takata</strong> &mdash; voted for the September hike &middot; dissented in October, formally proposing <strong>1.5%</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>His stated reason is the interesting part: with underlying inflation having reached the target, the Bank has entered a new phase in which it should move faster toward a neutral rate that leaves room to respond. His proposal was voted down.</p>

<p>So a board member read the Bank's own new sentence and concluded it implied a faster path. The majority read the same sentence and held.</p>

<p>Worth a caveat: the motion changed too. Voting against a rate rise in September and for a hold in October is not necessarily a change of mind. But Takata's is, and he spelled it out.</p>

<h2>The equity selling doubled</h2>

<p>This passed with no dissent at all, and it is the decision with the largest number attached.</p>

<p>The Bank spent years buying equity ETFs and now holds a very large position it has been unwinding since January at a deliberately unremarkable pace. From January 2027 that pace rises by about two and a half times:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Old pace</strong> &mdash; about &yen;330bn a year at book value &middot; roughly 0.05% of market turnover</li>
  <li><strong>New pace</strong> &mdash; about <strong>&yen;860bn a year</strong> at book value &middot; roughly 0.1% of turnover</li>
  <li><strong>At market value</strong> &mdash; the Bank's own footnote puts the new pace at about <strong>&yen;2.46 trillion a year</strong></li>
  <li><strong>J-REITs</strong> &mdash; unchanged at about &yen;5bn a year</li>
</ul>

<p>The Bank's reasoning is procedural: the selling has gone smoothly, trading volumes have grown, so the same share of the market now supports a larger number. Nobody voted against it.</p>

<p>Two issues ago this newsletter found that household equity holdings grew 47% in a year on valuation rather than buying. The largest single holder of Japanese equities has now decided to sell into that market at about two and a half times the previous rate.</p>

<h2>The forecast, and the line underneath it</h2>

<p>Medians of the board's projections, with July's in brackets:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>FY2026</strong> &mdash; GDP +0.7% [+0.6%] &middot; core CPI <strong>+2.5%</strong> [+2.5%]</li>
  <li><strong>FY2027</strong> &mdash; GDP +0.7% [+0.8%] &middot; core CPI <strong>+2.6%</strong> [+2.4%]</li>
  <li><strong>FY2028</strong> &mdash; GDP +0.8% [+0.8%] &middot; core CPI <strong>+2.0%</strong> [+2.0%]</li>
</ul>

<p>Three consecutive years at or above target, with FY2027 revised up two-tenths. Risks to the CPI outlook are described as skewed to the upside.</p>

<p>Now the footnote, which is where the actual uncertainty lives. The projections <em>exclude</em> a temporary cut to the consumption tax on food and drink currently before the Diet. If it passes as drafted, the Bank estimates it would take <strong>about 1.3 percentage points</strong> off FY2027 core CPI.</p>

<p>That would put the central forecast at roughly 1.3% &mdash; well under target &mdash; through a tax change rather than anything monetary. The Bank has declared the target met using a number a bill in parliament could halve.</p>

<h2>What the hold means downstairs</h2>

<p>For the household balance sheet this newsletter has been tracking, a hold means the recent past continues.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>The megabank ordinary deposit</strong> &mdash; 0.40%, set when the policy rate was 1.0% &middot; the banks never passed through the September rise, and now there is nothing new to pass through</li>
  <li><strong>The retail government bond</strong> &mdash; priced off a base rate that has stopped moving &middot; the variable ten-year still hands over two-thirds of it</li>
  <li><strong>Variable-rate mortgages</strong> &mdash; three-quarters of recent borrowers &middot; the five-year rule means they would not have felt a rise anyway, and now there is not one</li>
</ul>

<p>The one thing that did change is the only one households do not transact in directly. The Bank is selling more equities, and the equity block of the household balance sheet is the part that has been doing all the growing.</p>

<h2>One more number, from the same bank</h2>

<p>The Outlook says medium- to long-term inflation expectations "are at around 2 percent."</p>

<p>Seventeen days earlier the Bank published its household survey, which this newsletter covered last issue. Asked what prices would do over the next five years, households answered <strong>10.1% a year</strong> on the mean and <strong>5.0%</strong> on the median.</p>

<p>Both numbers are the Bank's own, and both are honest. Expectations are measured across markets, firms and households, and the footnote says many indicators sit near 2%. Households are simply not one of the ones that does.</p>

<p>Which is a reasonable way to run a central bank, and a strange thing to be true of a country. The institution setting the price of money believes inflation is 2%. The people holding &yen;1,132 trillion of deposits believe it is five times that. Policy is being set against the first number. Saving is being decided by the second.</p>
```
